THE QUESTION

Making science education more accessible led me to a difficult research question: how can language connect to coordinated movement? Sign-Translator explores that question while keeping generated motion distinct from linguistically validated American Sign Language.

01

More than a sequence of hand poses

Signing involves timing, body movement, facial expression, and linguistic structure. A model that produces plausible coordinates has not necessarily produced understandable language. That distinction shapes both the software interfaces and the claims I make about the work.

02

The experimental core

I built a synthetic-data baseline linking language and motion with graph neural networks, contrastive alignment, and conditional diffusion. Data-validation tools and reproducibility checks help test the behavior of the components without treating a passing software check as evidence of ASL fluency.

03

A research direction, with clear limits

The runnable baseline produces experimental motion representations. It is not a validated translator, and the project has not established ASL intelligibility through an independent qualified evaluation. The repository documents the gaps between the current experiment and the larger translation pipeline.

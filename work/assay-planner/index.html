ZENITHSYNC / EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN
THE QUESTION

Every measurement uses part of an experiment’s budget. I built a planner that considers what is already known, where uncertainty remains, and how a shared control affects several treatment measurements at once.

6,496paired assay contexts
100source patient IDs
32.82%lower MSE vs. random selection
01

The shared-control problem

A treatment response often depends on a measured control. When several responses share that reference, their errors are related. The planner retains this dependence instead of treating the control as perfectly known, and considers purchasing additional control measurements alongside treatment wells.

02

Choosing a batch

A Gaussian process models response contrasts and their uncertainty. A finite-pool optimizer then selects a batch of wells to reduce average model uncertainty across the planned doses. I connected this method to a local browser interface, a command-line workflow, and traceable processing of public data.

03

Testing the claim

The frozen retrospective confirmation covered 6,496 paired assay contexts representing 100 patient IDs. Exact selection reduced mean squared prediction error by 32.82% versus random selection under the same model. Against the stronger greedy baseline, the improvement was 0.66%—an important limit on the size of the advantage.

INSIDE THE ENGINEERING

The control well is part of the model.

  1. 01

    Keep the covariance

    Subtracting the same measured control from several treatment readings introduces shared error. With independent raw errors of variance s², the contrast-noise covariance is R = s²(I + 11ᵀ): 2s² on the diagonal and s² off it. The joint Gaussian model preserves that dependence.

  2. 02

    Search the finite design space

    The optimizer chooses a fixed-budget batch by reducing average posterior variance over target doses. Verified exchangeable wells can be grouped, allowing one representative per group-count pattern. This reduces enumeration without changing the optimum of the bounded problem.

  3. 03

    Freeze, then evaluate

    Model choices were fixed before confirmation on a separate public organoid study. Exact, greedy, and random policies share the same prediction model and measurement budget; patient-grouped analysis separates repeated assay contexts from independent patient IDs.

The covariance objective depends on the design and observed-well locations, not the response values. This is a fixed-model batch design; predictions update with measurements, but the selection rule is not response-adaptive.
